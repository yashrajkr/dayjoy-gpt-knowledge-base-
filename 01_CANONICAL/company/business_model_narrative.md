## 1. Business Model Overview

**VERIFIED:** Dayjoy describes itself as a **direct selling company** that sells wellness, nutrition, beauty, and home products through its official website and a network of independent distributors. [web:4][web:16][web:20]

**VERIFIED:** The company earns revenue primarily from **product sales** to end customers, both directly and via its distributor network. [web:4][web:20]

## 13. SWOT Analysis

### Strengths

- Clear direct-selling positioning with a distributor-led model. [web:4][web:20]
- Broad product-category coverage across wellness, beauty, home, and nutrition. [web:16][web:17]
- Official website provides visible customer support and grievance contacts. [web:7]
- Corporate identity details are publicly traceable. [web:5][web:12]

### Weaknesses

- Limited public financial transparency. [web:5][web:13]
- Many public claims are promotional and not independently verified. [web:2][web:14][web:26]
- Leadership structure and governance details not fully documented. [web:8][web:13]
- Product catalog depth and compensation-plan specifics not comprehensively published. [web:4][web:26]

### Opportunities

- Build stronger public trust through clearer documentation of products, quality standards, and certifications. [web:26]
- Publish a more complete FAQ, distributor policy, and compliance center. [web:4][web:7]
- Improve SEO and structured content around categories, brands, and customer journeys. [web:16][web:17]
- Standardize official corporate and social channels. [web:1][web:11]

### Risks

- Regulatory, reputational, and consumer-trust risks associated with direct selling. [web:4][web:20]
- Promotional statements may create credibility risk if not substantiated. [web:2][web:14][web:26]
- Reliance on distributor-generated content can cause inconsistent messaging. [web:11][web:26]
- Limited public financial disclosure makes external assessment difficult. [web:5][web:13]
